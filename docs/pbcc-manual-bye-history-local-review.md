## Finding and reproduction

The Next Game dialog sends checked **session-player row IDs** in `selectedSessionPlayerIds`. The action route loads all joined players, splits them into checked players and unchecked manual byes, and previously called `createNextRoundRobinRound` with only the checked players. With ten joined players and two unchecked, the planner therefore saw eight players and returned zero planner byes. The route appended the two manual byes only when persisting `round_robin_matches.bye_players` on the first court row. The saved records represented the byes correctly, but the current round and subsequent manually selected rounds were planned as eight-player subsets, losing the full ten-player partner, group, court, and bye context. If the next round checked all ten, the saved byes were read by `nightlyHistory`, but that did not repair the earlier eight-player planning decision.

An exact local route-equivalent reproduction before the correction reported `joined: 10`, `selected: 8`, `plannerByes: []`, `savedByes: [0,1]`. A full-night test that repeatedly prescribed a valid saved bye sequence exposed a 4/2 court split despite a known valid complete-night sequence. The history reader also initialized historical trio, quartet, adjacency, and three-appearance streak counters to zero; it retained `last`, `previous`, and `groups`, so future overlap choices could see the saved groups, but its quality totals omitted prior exposures.

## Scoped correction

For a non-ladder Next Game with exactly ten joined players, eight checked players, two manually selected byes, and two usable courts, the route now passes all ten joined players in original roster order and the two selected player IDs to the existing whole-night planner. The planner requires that pair in the immediate round, while still considering all saved matches and all ten players. The route persists the planner's two byes once. Other attendance sizes, ladder play, and one-court selection retain their existing path.

The planner validates that both forced IDs are distinct joined players. It bypasses automatic low-bye-count filtering for the user's immediate selection; later automatic choices retain the prior fairness rule. It keeps the first round's bye pair fixed during group improvement. A verified saved-game prefix or a fresh complete-night plan is reused when its next bye pair agrees with the manual selection; a different manual pair uses the bounded continuation search. Saved matches are never edited by planning. `nightlyHistory` now reconstructs historical quartet, trio, adjacency, and streak totals from the actual saved court groups before evaluating a continuation.

The hard constraint in generation is **no newly repeated partner**; if the bounded search finds no such continuation, it returns the existing explicit planning-limit error instead of assigning a repeated partner. A manual ten-player round has exactly two distinct byes. Even bye totals, court-use difference at most one, avoidance of consecutive trios/quartets, and spacing shared-court pairs are balancing objectives within the bounded search, not guarantees against arbitrary edited history. The automatic low-bye-count candidate filter favors fairness; a user-prescribed pair overrides it for that round. A prior manual choice can make 1–2 final byes or a one-game court difference mathematically impossible; the planner preserves that history and reports remaining imbalance.
